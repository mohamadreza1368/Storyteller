# Book Writer Skill: Master Author Craft & Scene Tension

## Overview
This skill governs narrative craft, physical staging, and scene-level dramatic momentum for long-form literary fiction.

## Core Directives

### 1. Show, Don't Tell
- Never announce or summarize a character's emotional state (e.g. avoid "he felt a sudden wave of dread" or "a surge of wonder washed over her").
- Ground emotion strictly in physical action, tactile temperature, involuntary muscle tension, the cadence of breathing, and the silence between spoken words.

### 2. Narrative Perspective & Voice
- Strictly Third-Person Limited: Anchor every scene inside the designated POV character's direct sensory perception.
- Never head-hop between minds within the same scene.
- Weave internal tactical calculations seamlessly into prose without artificial italic tags or quotation marks.

### 3. Scene Arc & Tension Progression
- Each scene must follow a dynamic tension curve:
  1. Beginning Complication / Sensory Anchor
  2. Escalating Interpersonal Friction / Obstacle
  3. Critical Turn, Revelation, or Reversal
- Avoid generic obstacles; make conflicts immediate, personal, and physically tangible.

### 4. Dynamic Sentence Rhythm
- Alternate deliberately between short, punchy sentences for immediate action or tension, and measured, sensory-rich sentences for reflection.
- Keep punctuation clean and hygienic: no stacked punctuation (`;;`, `.;`), minimal semicolons, and natural clause division.
