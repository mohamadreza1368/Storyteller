Phase 1: Setting Premise, Historical Era & Chapter Budget

Anchoring the narrative world and establishing physical reality

The story world is the physical foundation that anchors all autonomous interactions. When creating a novel:

Short Story Architecture (3 to 6 Chapters)
To prevent repetitive wandering and endless loops, stories must be designed as focused short novels or novellas (3 to 6 chapters). Every chapter has a defined milestone in the overarching arc:
  • Chapter 1: Inciting incident, arrival of characters at the scene, and initial clash of goals.
  • Middle Chapters: Escalation of conflict, discovery of clues, shifting alliances.
  • Final Chapter: Dramatic climax and definitive conclusion of the central premise.
Strict Chapter Word Cap (Under 3,000 Words)
Under no circumstances should any single chapter exceed 3,000 words. The engine targets 1,500 to 2,500 words of dense, literary prose. This preserves narrative momentum and avoids conversational bloat.
Grounding Historical Era & Custom Locations
Always specify an exact historical year or era (e.g., 1894 Victorian Edinburgh or 2045 Cyberpunk London). The engine strictly locks in custom locations and describes them using concrete, tactile physical details (wet slate, cold iron, tallow candles).
1-Click Visual Presets & AI Idea Co-Pilot
If you want an instant start, select one of the curated visual presets (Mystery & Crime, Romance, Sci-Fi) or type a single-sentence idea into the Idea Co-Pilot to have the world rules, characters, and plotlines forged automatically.
2

Phase 2: Casting Autonomous Characters (Main vs. Supporting)

Isolated character minds with independent LLM system prompts

In Storyteller AI, characters are not flat puppets or generic placeholders. Each character operates as an independent LLM instance with their own dedicated, isolated System Prompt.

The Motivational Engine:
• External Want: The tangible objective they chase (e.g. recovering the stolen ledger).
• Internal Need: The psychological void they must resolve to mature.
• Fatal Flaw: The blindspot that creates friction with other characters.
• Buried Secret: The hidden truth they must protect at all costs.
Zero Telepathy / Isolated Knowledge:

Characters think and act strictly from their own vantage point. They have zero telepathic access to another character's secret thoughts, motivations, or off-screen actions unless disclosed openly in spoken dialogue. This creates authentic subtext, suspense, and organic conflict.

Complete Supporting Character Preservation:

Whether designated as Main or Supporting, every character you specify in the cast is strictly preserved and carried through the narrative matrix and scene blocking. Supporting characters will not be erased or forgotten.

Phase 3: Scene Directing & Multi-Agent Live Dialogue Simulation

Live turn-by-turn conversational interaction and tension arcs

Before writing continuous chapter prose, the engine directs the dramatic action:

Scene Tension Mapping
The Scene Director deconstructs each chapter into 2 to 3 dynamic scenes. Each scene has a tangible location, a distinct POV character, an immediate dramatic obstacle, and an outcome that decisively shifts the interpersonal balance.
Turn-by-Turn Multi-Agent Exchange
Characters engage in multi-turn dialogues where each turn produces:
  • Physical Action: Observable body language and gestures.
  • Spoken Dialogue: Spoken addressing other present characters.
  • Secret Thought: Private calculations hidden from others.
4

Phase 4: Master Prose Synthesis & Anti-AI Quality Standards

Enforcing master author craftsmanship, humanizer rules, and banned cliché elimination

The Master Narrator weaves the simulated scenes and dialogue into literary chapters. It integrates three state-of-the-art writing craft skills:

1. Master Fiction Author Craft (from book-writer-skill):

• Show, Don't Explain: Never announce emotions directly (e.g. avoid *"he felt a surge of fear"*). Instead, show physical sensations, tactile weight, and silence.
• Strict Third-Person Limited: Thoughts are woven directly into prose without quotation marks or italics.
• Punctuation Discipline: Em dashes (—) are used sparingly (max 1-2 per scene for abrupt interruptions).

2. Elimination of AI Tells (from blader/humanizer):

• No "Not X, but Y": Forbids artificial negative/positive balancing acts (e.g. *"It was not just a city, it was a living beast"*).
• No Trailing Closers: Forbids dramatic one-line conclusions (e.g. *"That was the real danger."*).
• No Fake-Deep Aphorisms: Forbids generic philosophical filler (e.g. *"At its core..."*).
• No Forced Triads: Forbids grouping three adjectives or clauses for artificial cadence.

3. Forbidden AI Cliché Vocabulary (from avoid-ai-writing):

The automated post-processor inspects and replaces dead AI words: delve, tapestry, testament, beacon, palpable, labyrinthine, dance of shadows, whispers of, chill ran down, air was thick with, time stood still, crescendo, inexorably. All text is grounded in concrete sensory reality.

Frequently Asked Questions (FAQ)

Q: Why did my previous story drift or repeat?

Repetition usually occurs when the story lacks a specific historical era/year or when the chapter budget is too high. By planning for 3 to 6 chapters, locking custom locations, and providing distinct character secrets, the engine maintains razor-sharp forward momentum.

Q: How does the "Auto-Continue" toggle work?

When Auto-Continue is enabled, the studio automatically generates the next chapter as soon as the previous chapter finishes, continuing until the final chapter is written. If you prefer to review each chapter individually or edit the cast between episodes, you can pause or stop Auto-Continue at any time.

Q: Can I view character thoughts and scene staging?

Yes! In the Novel view, click the "Behind the Scenes" button on any chapter card to open the drawer and read the exact multi-turn dialogue rounds, physical actions, and secret internal thoughts of each character.

Overview: How an Autonomous Novel is Created

Storyteller AI operates on an autonomous 6-stage narrative engine. Unlike conventional LLM chatbots that produce generic text summaries in a single shot, Storyteller simulates independent character minds, blocks cinematic scenes, and synthesizes publication-grade chapters.

01
Step 1: Premise, World & Era Setting

Provide your title, logline, genre, and world environment. Be specific with historical years (e.g., Victorian Edinburgh, 1894 or 2045 London) and custom locations. These are strictly locked into the narrative anchor.

02
Step 2: Casting Main & Supporting Characters

Define each character's role, external tangible want, internal emotional need, fatal blindspot, and buried secret. The engine guarantees complete preservation of supporting characters across chapters.

03
Step 3: Multi-Agent Live Dialogue Simulation

Before a chapter is written, the Scene Director breaks the chapter into 2 to 3 dramatic scenes. Characters engage in turn-by-turn dialogue where each agent outputs a physical action, spoken words, and secret internal thoughts.

04
Step 4: Master Prose Synthesis & Anti-AI Quality Gate

The Master Narrator weaves the simulated scenes and dialogue turns into expansive prose. An automated post-processor cleans all AI tells (no "Not X but Y", no forced triads, no cliché words like tapestry or delve).

Autonomous Character Simulation (No Telepathy)

Every character in your novel is powered by a dedicated LLM instance with an isolated System Prompt. They act, speak, and make deductions strictly from their own point of view.

The Motivational Engine
• External Want: The tangible objective they chase (e.g., recovering a stolen ledger).
• Internal Need: The psychological void they must resolve.
• Fatal Flaw: The blindspot that causes conflict with others.
• Buried Secret: The secret they must guard at all costs.
Multi-Turn Conversational Rounds
When characters are placed in a scene together, they respond directly to the previous character's spoken words and body language. They speak with human brevity, interruptions, and natural hesitations.

Integrated Writing Craft & Humanizer Rules

The storytelling engine enforces the literary principles from book-writer-skill, humanizer, and avoid-ai-writing at every generation stage:

1. Banned AI Clichés & Words:
Forbidden: delve, tapestry, testament, beacon, palpable, labyrinthine, dance of shadows, whispers of, chill ran down, air was thick with, time stood still, little did they know, crescendo, inexorably. Replaced with concrete physical descriptions (wet mortar, cold iron, quiet breath).
2. Elimination of AI Structural Habits:
• No "Not X, but Y": Forbid artificial contrasting formulas (e.g. "It was not merely cold, but freezing").
• No Trailing Closers: Forbid dramatic one-line conclusions (e.g. "And nothing would ever be the same.").
• No Forced Triads: Forbid artificial groupings of three adjectives or three clauses for fake rhythm.
3. Show, Don't Explain & Punctuation Discipline:
Thoughts are woven directly into third-person limited prose without italics or quotation marks. Em dashes (—) are used sparingly (max 1-2 per scene strictly for genuine interruption).

Short Story Architecture & Chapter Limits

Storyteller is optimized for short, impactful stories where every chapter pushes the plot forward without filler.

Target: 3 to 6 Chapters Total
• Chapter 1: Inciting incident, arrival at location, initial clash of objectives.
• Chapters 2-(N-1): Escalation, discoveries, secret pressures, shifting alliances.
• Final Chapter: Climax, central conflict resolution, and definitive closure.
Strict Word Cap: Under 3,000 Words
Every chapter aims for 1,500 to 2,500 words and is strictly constrained to never exceed 3,000 words. This ensures tight narrative momentum, sharp dialogue, and memorable reading.
